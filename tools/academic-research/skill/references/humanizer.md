# Academic Humanizer Reference

Use this for academic biology, ecology, thesis, and manuscript prose when the
user asks to humanize, polish, or make text sound less AI-generated.

## Core Principle

Write like a careful scientist explaining the work clearly. Do not write like a
marketing page, a software README, or a model trying to sound human. The target
is scientific clarity with a natural rhythm.

## Rules

- Do not use em dashes.
- Do not add filler to sound human.
- Keep correct technical terms.
- Define technical terms on first use when useful for non-specialist readers.
- Remove generic AI wording such as "seamlessly integrates", "robust
  framework", "valuable insights", "unlocking potential", "maximize
  accessibility", and "ensure full traceability" unless the sentence gives
  specific evidence.
- Prefer prose over bullet lists in manuscript text.
- Preserve reasoning, caveats, attribution, and reproducibility details.
- Make claims specific and defensible.
- Do not invent citations or imply a source supports a claim without checking.

## Preserve Precise Terms

Keep standard terms when they are scientifically useful, including occurrence
records, coordinate uncertainty, spatial thinning, presence-only data,
background points, accessible area, target-group background, habitat
suitability, species distribution modelling, taxonomic curation, data source,
and data provenance.

For species distribution models, describe outputs as relative habitat
suitability unless the model truly estimates occurrence probability.

## Final Check

Before returning revised manuscript prose, check that:

- generic AI phrases are gone
- technical terms are preserved or defined
- claims match the evidence
- caveats still prevent overclaiming
- prose is concise without becoming cryptic
