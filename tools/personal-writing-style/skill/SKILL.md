# Personal writing style

Write precise, natural prose that makes its meaning clear on the first reading. Preserve the facts, distinctions, uncertainty, citations, and technical terms that matter. Use concrete nouns and direct verbs. Let evidence carry the importance of a claim. Include enough detail for the reader to understand the work, then place that detail where it helps rather than interrupts.

The guidance here is enough for short and routine writing. For substantial drafting, style-sensitive revision, or review, read [voice-evidence.md](references/voice-evidence.md). For scientific or academic work, also read [scientific-writing.md](references/scientific-writing.md). Use [anti-slop-audit.md](references/anti-slop-audit.md) for a substantial rewrite or final polish. Read [review-workflow.md](references/review-workflow.md) when the user wants to inspect and approve changes.

## Write from meaning

Before drafting, identify the genre, reader, purpose, and requested output. Make an internal inventory of the claims, quantities, names, citations, comparisons, scope conditions, and uncertainty. Preserve their meaning, status, and strength.

State the relationship clearly before evaluating it. Keep related ideas together. Prefer the shortest wording that preserves the intended distinction, but let accuracy set the limit on brevity. Cut connective padding and empty emphasis. Keep useful repetition when the familiar noun is clearer than a synonym, compressed label, or pronoun.

Use one stable term for one object. Keep established specialist vocabulary when it is the clearest language for the intended reader. When a term comes from another discipline, briefly explain what it does in this study or document. Match the depth and location of technical detail to the reader and the job of the section. Move necessary detail to Methods, an appendix, or a later paragraph when it would interrupt the main point.

Ground judgments in observations, comparisons, or consequences. Describe the finished subject rather than accidental drafting or implementation history. Keep development history when the genre calls for it, as in release notes, progress reports, migrations, experiment records, and debugging explanations.

Keep personality grounded in the source. Preserve genuine opinions, anecdotes, emphasis, uncertainty, irritation, and warmth when the genre allows them. Avoid polishing away useful irregularity. A restrained scientific passage can still have a human voice through the distinctions it protects and the details it chooses to explain.

## Respect the user's defaults

- Avoid em dashes. Reshape the sentence or use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses. Keep en dashes when they correctly mark ranges or established compounds.
- Avoid semicolons. Prefer a period, comma, colon, or a clearer sentence boundary.
- Prefer affirmative guidance and useful next steps. Reserve prohibitions and blockers for real constraints.
- Infer preferences cautiously. Explicit feedback outranks inferred patterns, and later feedback outranks earlier guidance.
- Treat an accepted edit without comment as weak evidence. A direct explanation or repeated correction is stronger.
- Leave clear prose alone. Revision should solve a real problem rather than display effort.

When a claim is ambiguous, inconsistent, or scientifically doubtful, keep its wording during a strict style edit and flag the issue separately. During substantive work, identify the uncertainty and request the missing fact or mark a clear placeholder. A smooth sentence is not worth a false claim.

## Deliver the requested thing

For a draft or rewrite, begin with clean prose. For a review, lead with the most consequential findings and show concrete revisions. Keep explanations proportional to the decision. Preserve ordinary courtesies in correspondence when they support the relationship or invite a needed reply.

If another writing or humanizer skill is active, the user's explicit preferences and the factual safeguards here take precedence.

Before delivery, check that every claim retains its meaning and status, the intended reader can follow the relationships, every sentence contributes, and the prose sounds specific rather than generic or self-congratulatory. Search once for em dashes and semicolons.
