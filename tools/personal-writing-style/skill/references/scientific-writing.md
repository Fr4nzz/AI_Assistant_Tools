# Scientific and academic writing

Use this layer for manuscripts, theses, proposals, abstracts, reports, captions, and responses to reviewers.

## Protect the science

Preserve every number, citation, taxon, direction of effect, comparison, caveat, and scope condition. Keep planned work grammatically distinct from completed work. When the source is ambiguous or conflicts with the implementation, retain the uncertainty and flag it for resolution. Accuracy takes priority over a fluent but unsupported repair.

Use stable technical terms consistently. Repetition helps when a synonym could imply a different variable, group, dataset, or method. Passive voice works when the actor is irrelevant, the object deserves emphasis, or the discipline expects it. Hedging carries information when it marks uncertainty or limits the scope of a claim.

## Write for ecologists

Use the field's ordinary nouns. Keep concise ecological terms that the intended readers know, including terms such as “phenology”. Explain a machine-learning, statistical, or computational term through its role in the study when that connection may be unfamiliar. The explanation should help the reader understand what was measured, how the method works here, or why the choice affects interpretation.

Separate stable scientific names from internal development labels. In the manuscript, use the verified publication name or a functional description. Keep experiment names in provenance records and reproducibility mappings when they are needed to trace the result.

Name biological quantities precisely. Abundance, number of families, and community composition are distinct outcomes. Plain language should reveal these distinctions rather than merge them behind a broad label.

State the setting, observation, or mechanism before evaluating its importance. Place citations close to the claims they support.

## Methods

Write Methods so another researcher can understand and reproduce the work. Ordered steps, lists, and repeated terms are useful when they make the procedure easier to follow. Include the rationale and grouping structure needed to evaluate each non-obvious choice.

The same detail may need different treatment elsewhere. A proposal paragraph can name a random effect and leave its full grouping rationale for Methods. A Discussion can state the implication and point to the analysis without repeating its implementation. Move technical explanation to the place where it does its job, while preserving everything needed for accuracy and reproducibility.

Describe the intended final procedure rather than the sequence of experiments that produced it. Keep unfinished work grammatically planned. Include model names, paths, counts, and implementation details when they are verified and relevant to reproducibility.

## Structure

Build each paragraph around one developing point. Put shared methods together, and keep objective-specific methods or interpretations with the objective they serve. Use transitions when they reveal a relationship. Omit reminders to earlier sections when the connection is already clear.

Treat prose editing and scientific verification as separate passes. Preserve values and citations during the prose pass, then flag any item that needs factual checking.
